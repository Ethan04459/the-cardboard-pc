Real‑World Applications

The same components in your cardboard model power everything from games to AI to careers.

🕹️
Gaming Performance
High FPS · Better visuals · Smooth gameplay

Modern games rely heavily on the GPU for rendering graphics, lighting, shadows, and physics.

  • GPU Higher FPS, smoother gameplay, and better visual quality
  • CPU Handles game logic, AI, and physics calculations
  • RAM Reduces stuttering and improves loading times
🎬
Video Editing & Content Creation
Premiere Pro · DaVinci Resolve · After Effects

Editing software demands serious hardware to render, export, and play back large video files.

  • CPU Rendering, exporting, and timeline performance
  • GPU Effects, colour grading, and real-time playback
  • SSD Fast loading of large video files
🤖
AI, Machine Learning & Data
Neural networks · Image recognition · Simulations

AI workloads use GPUs because they can process thousands of operations in parallel.

  • GPU Accelerates neural networks and simulations
  • CPU Handles control logic and data organisation
  • SSD Reduces dataset loading times

Everyday Computing
Browsing · Multitasking · Daily productivity

Even normal tasks depend on good hardware working together behind the scenes.

  • CPU Smooth multitasking across apps
  • RAM Keeps apps open without lag
  • SSD Instant boot times and fast app launches
  • GPU Smooth video playback and UI animations
🎮
Esports & Competitive Play
200–300 FPS · Low latency · No bottlenecks

Competitive players push hardware to the limit — every millisecond counts.

  • GPU Pushes 200–300 FPS for high-refresh monitors
  • CPU Avoids bottlenecks that cap frame rates
  • RAM Low latency for responsive input

Professional Workstations
Engineering · Architecture · Science

Industries depend on powerful hardware to model, simulate, and calculate at scale.

  • GPU 3D modelling and complex simulations

  • RAM Handles huge datasets in memory
  • SSD Rapid project loading with NVMe speeds
The Project

Built from cardbaord,
powered by curiosity

Cardboard PC is a physical, scale model of a real desktop computer — every major component reproduced using cardboard and craft materials.

The goal: make the insides of a computer tangible and easy to understand for anyone, without needing to take apart expensive hardware.

Each labelled component explains its role, how it connects to others, and why it matters — just like this website.
