Where it all began

Introduction

Have you ever wondered what's actually inside a computer? Most people use computers every day — but very few have ever seen the components that make them work. We wanted to change that. Our project, Cardboard PC, set out to build a full-scale physical model of a desktop computer using nothing but cardboard and craft materials.

Our goal was simple: make the inside of a PC accessible, understandable, and tangible for everyone — no technical background needed. By recreating each component by hand, we wanted people to be able to look at our model and immediately understand what each part does, how it connects to the rest of the system, and why it matters.

This website is an extension of that model — a digital companion that lets you explore every component in detail, see how they apply to real-world tasks, and understand why the hardware inside a computer is so important.

What we achieved

Conclusion

Through the Cardboard PC project, we successfully built a complete, labelled model of a desktop computer — and in doing so, proved that understanding technology doesn't require expensive equipment or a classroom. It just takes curiosity and a willingness to build.

Every component we recreated — from the CPU to the case fans — helped us understand not just what each part does, but why it matters and how it fits into the bigger picture of a working computer. The process of building it forced us to research, collaborate, and explain ideas clearly, which deepened our own understanding along the way.

We hope that anyone who interacts with our project — whether at the STEM Fair or on this website — walks away with a clearer picture of what's inside the machine they use every day. If we've made computers even a little less mysterious, then we've done our job.
